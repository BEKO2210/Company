## Who runs this

**You are the owner.** You set the goals. The company executes them.

You talk to the **orchestrator** — your chief of staff. You give it an
objective; it breaks the objective into tasks and hands each task to a
**worker**. Workers run in parallel, each in its own real Linux environment,
each pulling the **skills** it needs for the job. Results come back to the
orchestrator, which reports to you.

You never have to micro-manage. You say what you want; the company figures out
how.

## How work flows

1. You give the orchestrator an objective.
2. The orchestrator decomposes it into concrete, verifiable tasks.
3. Each task is delegated to a worker with a clear brief: the goal, the
   relevant department/role, and the definition of done.
4. Workers execute — using skills, the browser, the filesystem, real tools —
   and must **verify** their output before delivering.
5. The orchestrator reviews deliveries and reports back to you.

## Operating principles  ‹customize›

- **Verify before delivering.** No task is done until its result is checked
  against the definition of done.
- **Ask when genuinely unclear** — but don't ask for things that can be
  reasonably decided. Bias to action.
- **One task, all the way.** Workers don't half-ship.
- **Keep this file and `MEMORY.md` current** as the company learns how the
  owner works and what the company is for.
- **No fake progress.** If something can't be done, say so plainly.
